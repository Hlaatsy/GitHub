## Who this is sold to

Organisations, not individuals. Marketing and PR teams, internal comms, HR and
training, at companies that already spend real money on video and already have
a compliance function that asks where a person's likeness went.

That decision changes the pricing more than it changes the product. A
solopreneur weighing R149 against their airtime is a different buyer from a
marketing manager with a production budget, comparing us against the
R40 000-R80 000 it costs to book a crew, a presenter and a studio day — and to
do it again when the policy changes.

Against that anchor the entry price is not the objection. Trust, consent,
delivery and somebody answering the phone are what decide it.

Individual self-serve stays available, but as the way a buyer tries the
product before a real conversation, not as the business.

### Why avatars are capped

An organisation cannot add unlimited avatars, and only the first reason is
about money:

- **Each one is a guided build** — recording direction, a clean voice sample,
  iterations. That is human time, and we sell it as Avatar Setup at R2 500.
- **A cloned voice carries a per-avatar cost** with the provider, unlike a
  stock voice.
- **Every avatar is a real person's likeness.** An uncapped account is an
  uncapped consent surface: more faces on file than anyone is tracking, which
  is exactly the failure the compliance pillar exists to prevent. A cap is
  what makes the consent register finite enough to actually maintain.

Extra avatars beyond the plan are sold as Avatar Setup, or the account moves
up a tier. Enterprise negotiates the number, because a bank presenting in
four languages with a different face per division genuinely needs more than
five — and at that size the consent register is part of the contract.

Every video up to 2 minutes. Cost per video falls at each step — R49.90,
R37.48, R33.33 — which is the rate that matters when a buyer is costing a
campaign.

### Team 5 — the marketing and PR bundle

Five seats, because that is the shape of the team that buys this: a marketing
manager, a PR lead, a social or content person, and one or two in internal
comms or HR who need training video without going through marketing every
time.

What those seats get that the tiers below do not:

- **Shared brand avatars.** One presenter used by all five, rather than five
  people each building their own and the brand drifting.
- **Shared asset library** — scripts, avatars, voices, past videos in one place
  instead of on five laptops.
- **Approval workflow.** Nothing publishes under the company name without a
  named person approving it. This is often what decides the purchase, because
  it is what the communications lead is accountable for.
- **A consent register across the team**, so the compliance answer covers the
  organisation rather than one user.

The per-seat price rises here, R500 to R800, and that is deliberate. Each Team
seat carries 24 videos a month against Pro's 13, plus the collaboration
features above. The number that falls is the one the buyer budgets against:
cost per video.

### Enterprise

Custom, annual, negotiated. API access, custom corporate avatars, SLAs, a data
processing agreement, named support.

Price on the value of the alternative — a production agency on retainer — not
on video count. The self-serve arithmetic is irrelevant at this size.

Sell it last. An enterprise pilot that goes badly because the pipeline was not
ready costs more than the contract was worth, and these buyers talk to each
other.

## Avatar Setup

**R2 500 per avatar, once-off.**

The setup fee comes back, and here it belongs. A free build was right when the
buyer was weighing R149 against airtime. An organisation reads a free
implementation as a product with nobody behind it — a stated onboarding fee is
what a procurement process expects to see.

It is also real work: recording direction, a clean voice sample, iterations
until the avatar genuinely resembles the presenter, and a consent record filed
against the organisation. Included free on annual Team 5 and Enterprise
contracts, where the commitment covers it.

## Overage

Extra videos beyond the monthly allowance. One credit, one video.

| Pack | Price | Per video |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 extra | R79 | R79.00 |
| 10 | R690 | R69.00 |
| 25 | R1 475 | R59.00 |

**The rule still holds and still has to be checked:** overage runs R59-R79 a
video against a dearest tier rate of R49.90, so buying past the allowance is
always dearer than moving up a tier. Reversed, nobody upgrades — they run on
overage forever and the tiers stop meaning anything.

The consumer credit packs this replaces (R26.60-R35) broke that rule
immediately against B2B pricing: every one of them undercut every tier. Any
time a price moves, re-check the ordering before it ships.

Credits do not expire monthly — twelve months from purchase, stated at
purchase rather than in the terms page.

## Done-for-you ladder

The service layer is still where the business is, and against a B2B buyer it
is an easier sale than the software: they were going to pay someone for this
anyway.

| Offer | Proposed | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Content Engine** | R7 500/mo | 12 videos a month, scripted, produced, calendar |
| Content Engine+ | R12 500/mo | 30 videos, translation, priority turnaround |

Price against what it replaces. Twelve produced videos a month from an agency
is well past R7 500, and a single studio day with presenter and crew costs
more than a year of the subscription. The comparison the buyer makes is never
against a cheaper AI tool — it is against their current production line.

Cap revisions in writing. The cost here is script writing and client
back-and-forth, never compute, and uncapped revisions is how a good client
becomes an unprofitable one.

Every organisation putting a person's face into synthetic video has these

the room as the party that understands the risk rather than the party
creating it.

It is also the natural second conversation. A company that has just written an
AI content policy has, by definition, decided it is going to make AI content.

## The funnel

A B2B funnel, which is slower, lumpier and worth far more per customer.

1. **LinkedIn** (`projects/identical/queue/`) — the right channel for this
   buyer, and every post can be made with the product, so the advertisement
   is also the proof.
2. **A compliance conversation.** The consent pack or AI content policy, or
   just the question nobody in the room has answered yet. This gets you in
   as the party who understands the risk.
3. **A pilot.** One department, one avatar, a handful of videos. Avatar Setup at
   R2 500 is the first invoice and it prices the work honestly.
4. **A team subscription** once the pilot has an internal champion —
   Team 5 is sized for the department that ran it.
5. **Content Engine or Enterprise** when video becomes a standing commitment
   rather than a project.

Individual self-serve sits alongside this, not underneath it: a marketing
manager who wants to try the thing before putting it in front of their head
of communications. Treat a Starter signup from a company email as a lead, not
a customer.

### What to measure

Not monthly signups. In this model the numbers that matter are:

- **Pilots started**, and how many convert to a subscription.
- **Time from first contact to first invoice.** Expect months, not days. If
  you plan cashflow on consumer-style conversion you will be wrong in a
  direction that hurts.
- **Seats used per account.** A Team 5 account with one active seat is a
  renewal that will not happen, and it is visible long before the renewal
  date.
- **Which pillar opened the door** — compliance, cost-versus-crew, or
  language. That tells you which of the five in `positioning.md` to lead with
  next, and the answer may not be the one you expect.

## Before any of this is public

- **Cost a video properly.** Every tier rests on the per-video platform cost,
  which is still unknown — `app/provider.py` deliberately reports None for it.
  It matters less than it did at R149: at R49.90 a video on the entry tier and
  a buyer anchored to a R40 000 studio day, there is room. It still has to be
  a number rather than a hope.
- **Check the overage ordering** in every market, every time a price moves.
  Overage must stay dearer per video than every subscription tier. It broke
  the moment pricing changed, and it broke silently.
- **Seats need enforcing, not just advertising.** A five-seat plan with no
  seat limit is a one-seat plan the customer shares, and you find out at
  renewal. Avatar limits are now enforced in the app; seats are not, because
  team accounts do not exist yet — that is the next real piece of work if
  Team 5 is going to be the tier we lead with.
- **Contracts, not just checkout.** B2B buyers need an order form, payment
  terms, a revision cap on services, and an answer to what happens to avatars
  and source footage when the contract ends — that last one is a POPIA
  question before it is an admin one.
- **VAT.** Business buyers reclaim it, so quote excluding VAT and say so —
  the opposite of the consumer convention.
- **Payment rails** still matter, but differently: EFT and invoicing carry B2B
  here, not mobile money. Keep mobile money for self-serve.
